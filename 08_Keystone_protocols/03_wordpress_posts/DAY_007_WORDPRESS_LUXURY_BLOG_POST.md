<h2>The Clinical Science: Why Early GLP-1 Weight Loss is 70% Water</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To understand what happened inside your body during Week 1, we must look at human biochemistry and glycogen storage stoichiometry. In 1970, Swedish physiologists <strong>Karl-Erik Olsson and Bengt Saltin</strong> published a landmark study in <em>Acta Physiologica Scandinavica</em> (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5475323/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PMID: 5475323</a>) proving that <strong>1 gram of stored glycogen binds approximately 3 to 4 grams of intracellular water</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>An adult male carries roughly 500 grams of glycogen: 100 grams inside the liver and 400 grams tightly packed into skeletal muscle tissue. At a 3.5:1 water binding ratio, those 500 grams of glycogen hold nearly <strong>1.8 to 2.0 kilograms (~4.5 to 5.5 lbs)</strong> of intracellular water weight like a fully saturated sponge.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2>The Builder Translation: Dewatering an Excavation Site</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In residential construction across Vancouver and Squamish, building on steep granite hillsides means dealing with heavy coastal rainstorms. When torrential rain floods a 12-foot foundation excavation pit, thousands of gallons of standing brown stormwater pool on the clay floor.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before you can pour reinforced concrete footings, you drop a 3-inch submersible Honda trash pump into the pit. Within two hours, you pump out 5,000 gallons of muddy surface water. An inexperienced home client looks into the dry trench and cheers: <em>"Look how much foundation you built!"</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Experienced builders know better: <strong>Pumping surface water didn't move a single cubic yard of solid granite bedrock</strong>. All you did was dewater the site so the excavator's hydraulic rock breakers can start hammering the ledge inch by inch. Week 1 on Tirzepatide was simply firing up the trash pump. Week 2 is where the real construction begins.</p>
